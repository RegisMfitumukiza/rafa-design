# Studio Schema — Complete Admin Guide

**For:** Studio leadership, marketing, and content directors  
**Purpose:** Know how to manage the studio's public identity and narrative  
**Location:** `/admin → "05 // Studio 🏛️"`

---

## Overview

The Studio document is a **singleton** (only one exists) that controls the entire `/studio` page. It defines:

- Studio name, manifesto, and philosophy
- Design principles and working methods
- Studio imagery and cinematic film
- Historical timeline, awards, and client sectors
- Visitor information

**Total Tabs:** 5 sections  
**Estimated time to fill:** 30-45 minutes (usually done once, updated occasionally)  
**Difficulty:** Medium (strategic thinking required)

---

## Why the Studio Page Matters

The studio page communicates:

- ✅ Your firm's identity and values
- ✅ Professional credibility and awards
- ✅ Historical growth and evolution
- ✅ Team culture and expertise
- ✅ How visitors can experience your work
- ✅ Client sectors and project types

# 01 // CONTENT

### What This Tab Does

Defines the **core narrative** of your studio (name, manifesto, philosophy).

---

## Field 1: Studio Name

**Field Name:** `name` (Required)

**What to enter:**

```
Official name of your studio / firm.
Used throughout the page.
Part of legal identity.
```

**Examples:**

```
✅ GOOD:
├─ RAFA Design
├─ RAFA Design Studio
├─ RAFA Arquitetura
└─ RAFA

❌ AVOID:
├─ The Studio (too generic)
├─ Architecture & Design (too vague)
└─ RAFA Design RAFA Design (redundant)
```

**Tips:**

- Keep consistent with legal name
- Under 60 characters
- Used in headers and navigation

**Real Example:**

```
Studio Name: RAFA Design
```

---

## Field 2: Overline

**Field Name:** `overline` (Required, text)

**What to enter:**

```
Short descriptor with location.
Appears above studio name on page.
Gives context and place identity.
```

**Examples:**

```
✅ GOOD:
├─ "Architecture & Interior Design // Porto"
├─ "Design Studio // Portugal"
├─ "Atelier de Architecture // Oporto"
└─ "Practice // Porto, Portugal"

❌ AVOID:
├─ "We are a design studio" (awkward)
├─ Just the city (not enough context)
└─ No location reference (lost identity)
```

**Format:**

- Discipline or description + Location
- Use // as separator
- Include city/country

**Real Example:**

```
Overline: "Architecture & Interior Design // Porto"
```

---

## Field 3: Manifesto

**Field Name:** `manifesto` (Required, text)

**What to enter:**

```
One-line declaration of purpose.
Your studio's north star.
What you fundamentally believe.
```

**Examples:**

```
✅ GOOD:
├─ "We design buildings and spaces that respond to
   specific conditions of place."
├─ "Architecture emerges from dialogue with context,
   not imposed from above."
├─ "Great design improves how people live every day."
└─ "Material honesty and human scale define our practice."

❌ AVOID:
├─ "We are a design studio" (meaningless)
├─ "We provide architecture services" (generic)
└─ Too long or rambling (not a manifesto)
```

**Length:**

- 200-300 characters (under limit)
- 1-2 sentences maximum
- Powerful and memorable
- Authentic to your values

**Tips:**

- This should move people
- Reflect your true philosophy
- Avoid corporate jargon
- Be specific about what makes you different

**Real Example:**

```
Manifesto: "We design buildings and spaces that respond
to specific conditions of place—topography, climate, light,
materials, culture. Architecture is a dialogue with context,
not an imposition."
```

---

## Field 4: Introduction

**Field Name:** `introduction` (Required, text)

**What to enter:**

```
Substantial introduction to the studio.
Explains who you are and what you do.
First thing visitors read on /studio page.
Sets tone for entire page.
```

**Examples:**

```
Introduction 1 (450 chars):
"RAFA Design is an architecture and interior design studio
based in Porto, Portugal. We work across residential,
institutional, and commercial projects at scales ranging
from single homes to urban masterplans. Our approach is
rooted in deep understanding of place—the specific conditions
of landscape, climate, materials, and culture. We believe
that great architecture improves how people live. It emerges
from listening, research, and rigorous design thinking.
Every project is a unique response to its context."

---

Introduction 2 (480 chars):
"Founded in 2019, RAFA Design brings together architects
and designers with cumulative experience across Europe and
the developing world. We are committed to material honesty,
craft excellence, and creating buildings that respond to
their specific place. We work collaboratively with clients,
consultants, and craftspeople to navigate the complex process
from concept to completed building. Our portfolio includes
residential homes, adaptive reuse projects, institutional
buildings, and urban strategies."
```

**Length:**

- 400-500 characters
- 3-5 sentences
- Comprehensive but readable
- Sets context and values

**Writing tips:**

- Start with who you are
- Explain what makes you different
- Convey your values and approach
- Be authentic and grounded

**Real Example:**

```
Introduction: "RAFA Design is an architecture and interior
design studio based in Porto, Portugal. Founded in 2019,
we work across residential, institutional, and commercial
projects at scales ranging from single homes to urban
masterplans. Our practice is rooted in deep understanding

culture. We believe that great architecture improves how
people live. Every project is a unique response to its
context, guided by material honesty, craft excellence,
and rigorous design thinking."
```

## Field 5: Philosophy

**Field Name:** `philosophy` (Required, text)

**What to enter:**

```
Detailed statement of design philosophy.
Explains your approach and values.
Shown on studio page.
More substantial than manifesto.
```

**Examples:**

```
Philosophy 1 (550 chars):
"Our philosophy is grounded in the belief that architecture
is a form of place-making. Every building sits on a specific
site with unique conditions—topography, climate, light
patterns, existing structures, material traditions, cultural
context. Responsive design listens to these conditions rather
than imposing predetermined solutions. We study how light
moves through seasons. We understand local materials and
building traditions. We design buildings that will age
gracefully, their materials developing patina and character
over decades. We believe that quality craft and thoughtful
detailing define lived experience. The proportions of stairs,
the warmth of timber, the coolness of stone—these daily
moments shape how people feel in a space."

---

Philosophy 2 (580 chars):
"We design with conviction that human scale is paramount.
How a door opens, the height of a ceiling, the warmth of
materials underfoot—these are not minor details but central
to creating spaces where people thrive. We work
collaboratively across disciplines. Architecture, interior
design, landscape design, and specialized consultants work
in concert, not isolation. This integrated approach creates
coherence from concept through completion. We embrace both
digital and analog tools. Computational design helps us
test solutions rapidly. Hand-drawing and physical modeling
keep us connected to spatial reality. We believe the best
buildings are those that improve environmental performance
while enhancing human experience—not one at the expense
of the other."
```

**Length:**

- 500-600 characters
- 4-6 sentences
- Deep and thoughtful
- Specific about approach and values

**Tips:**

- Be genuine and passionate
- Explain "why" behind your approach
- Include concrete examples
- Differentiate from generic design philosophy

**Real Example:**

```
Philosophy: "Our philosophy is grounded in the belief that
architecture is a form of place-making. Every building sits
on a specific site with unique conditions—topography, climate,
light patterns, materials, cultural traditions. Responsive
design listens to these conditions rather than imposing
solutions. We study how light moves through seasons. We
understand local materials and building traditions. We design
buildings that age gracefully, developing character over
decades. Quality craft and thoughtful detailing define lived
experience—proportions of stairs, warmth of timber, texture
of stone. These daily moments shape how people feel in space.
We work collaboratively across disciplines so that
architecture, interiors, and landscape design work in concert,
creating coherence from concept through completion."
```

# 02 // PRINCIPLES & METHODS

### What This Tab Does

Defines your **design principles** and **working methods** (structured lists).

---

## Field 1: Principles / Beliefs

**Field Name:** `beliefs` (Optional, array)

**What to enter:**

```
3-5 core design principles.
Each has: Code + Title + Statement.
Fundamental beliefs that guide work.
```

**Structure:**

### Code (required)

```
Numbered identifier for principle.

Examples:
├─ "PRINCIPLE 01"
├─ "PRINCIPLE 02"
├─ "PRINCIPLE 03"
└─ "PRINCIPLE 04"

Format: "PRINCIPLE XX" (with number)
```

### Title (required)

```
Name of the principle.

Examples:
├─ "Responsive Design"
├─ "Material Honesty"
├─ "Human Scale"
├─ "Craft Excellence"
└─ "Place-Making"
```

### Statement (required)

```
One-sentence explanation of principle.

Examples:
├─ "Design responds to specific conditions of place
   rather than imposing generic solutions."
├─ "Materials are chosen for their authentic beauty
   and durability, not for appearance alone."
├─ "Buildings exist to improve how people live.
   Daily moments and human proportions matter."
├─ "Quality craftsmanship and precise detailing
   define the difference between good and great."
└─ "Architecture creates place and community,
   not just shelter."
```

---

### Complete Principles Example

```
Principle 1:
├─ Code: "PRINCIPLE 01"
├─ Title: "Responsive Design"
└─ Statement: "Design responds to specific conditions of place—
              topography, climate, light, materials, culture—
              rather than imposing generic solutions."

Principle 2:
├─ Code: "PRINCIPLE 02"
├─ Title: "Material Honesty"
└─ Statement: "Materials are chosen for their authentic nature
              and durability. We celebrate how they age and
              develop character over time."

Principle 3:
├─ Code: "PRINCIPLE 03"
├─ Title: "Human Scale"
└─ Statement: "Buildings exist to improve how people live.
              Daily moments—light in a kitchen, proportions
              of stairs—are paramount."

Principle 4:
├─ Code: "PRINCIPLE 04"
├─ Title: "Craft Excellence"
└─ Statement: "Quality craftsmanship and precise detailing
              define the difference between good and
              exceptional. We prioritize craft."
```

**Number of principles:**

- Minimum: 3 (establishes philosophy)
- Typical: 4-5 (comprehensive)
- Maximum: 6 (becomes overwhelming)

---

## Field 2: Working Methods

**Field Name:** `workingMethods` (Optional, array)

**What to enter:**

```
3-5 key methods / processes.
How you actually work.
Each has: Code + Title + Description.
```

**Structure:**

### Code (required)

```
Numbered identifier for method.

Examples:
├─ "METHOD 01"
├─ "METHOD 02"
├─ "METHOD 03"
└─ "METHOD 04"
```

### Title (required)

```
Name of the method.

Examples:
├─ "Research & Analysis"
├─ "Collaborative Design"
├─ "Iterative Refinement"
├─ "Integration"
└─ "Craft & Detail"
```

### Description (required)

```
Detailed explanation of how you work (400 chars max).

Examples:
├─ "We begin every project with months of research:
   site visits, climate analysis, material studies,
   client interviews, regulatory review. This research
   phase informs all subsequent design decisions."

├─ "We work with clients, consultants, and specialists
   from project start. Architecture doesn't exist in
   isolation. Integrated teams create coherent solutions
   where building, landscape, and interiors work in concert."

├─ "Design is refined through iteration. Concepts are
   tested digitally and through physical models. We
   present options, gather feedback, and refine. This
   cycle continues until design intent is crystal clear."

└─ "We detail buildings carefully. We work closely with
   craftspeople and builders to ensure design is
   constructible. We visit sites frequently and solve
   problems as they arise, maintaining design intent."
```

---

### Complete Working Methods Example

```
Method 1:
├─ Code: "METHOD 01"
├─ Title: "Research & Analysis"
└─ Description: "Every project begins with deep research.
                We study the site's topography, climate,
                light patterns, existing structures, and
                material traditions. We interview clients,
                understand their values, analyze regulatory
                context. This research phase informs all
                subsequent design decisions."

Method 2:
├─ Code: "METHOD 02"
├─ Title: "Collaborative Design"
└─ Description: "Architecture doesn't happen in isolation.
                We work with landscape architects, interior
                designers, engineers, and specialized
                craftspeople. This integration ensures
                coherence from concept through completion."

Method 3:
├─ Code: "METHOD 03"
├─ Title: "Iterative Refinement"
└─ Description: "Design is refined through iteration. We
                develop concepts, test them through digital
                models, renderings, and physical mock-ups.
                We gather feedback, refine direction. This
                iterative cycle continues until design
                intent is unambiguous."

Method 4:
├─ Code: "METHOD 04"
├─ Title: "Craft & Detailing"
└─ Description: "We detail buildings with precision. We
                work closely with craftspeople and builders
                to ensure design is fully constructible.
                We visit sites frequently during
                construction and solve challenges while
                maintaining design intent."
```

**Number of methods:**

- Typical: 3-4 methods
- Can be up to 5-6
- Should cover entire project lifecycle

## Field 1: Studio Atelier / Practice Overview Image

**Field Name:** `atelierImage` (Optional, image)

**What to enter:**

```
Official photograph of your studio space.
Your atelier, workspace, practice environment.
Shows studio culture and atmosphere.
```

**What makes a good studio image:**

```
✅ GOOD:
├─ Authentic studio photograph (not staged)
├─ Shows workspace, materials, models, activity
├─ Captures studio atmosphere and culture
├─ High quality professional photography
├─ Wide shot showing studio layout
└─ Natural or professional lighting

❌ AVOID:
├─ Generic office photo
├─ Too formal/sterile (not authentic)
├─ Low resolution or blurry
├─ Only people (not the space)
└─ Marketing/stock photo feel
```

**Examples:**

```
GOOD studio photos show:
├─ Design team working on models or drawings
├─ Material samples and architectural models
├─ Detail of workspace with projects in progress
├─ Natural light coming into studio
├─ Tools, sketches, and creative artifacts
└─ Authentic moment of studio in action
```

**Sub-fields:**

### Alt Text (accessibility)

```
Description for screen readers.

Example:
"RAFA Design studio in Porto showing team at work
with architectural models and material samples"
```

### Caption (optional)

```
Text displayed under image.

Example:
"RAFA Design studio in Porto"

"Studio workspace and atelier"
```

### Credit (optional)

```
Photographer attribution.

Example:
"Photography by João Silva"
```

# BEST PRACTICES

**Writing your narrative:**

- ✅ Be authentic, not corporate-speak
- ✅ Show your philosophy through action, not buzzwords
- ✅ Include specific examples
- ✅ Balance aspiration with humility
- ✅ Let your voice come through

**Photography & film:**

- ✅ Professional quality images
- ✅ Authentic studio atmosphere (not staged)
- ✅ Film should be short and compelling (under 2 min ideal)
- ✅ Consistent style with rest of website

**Principles vs. Methods:**

- Principles = what you believe
- Methods = how you actually do it
- Both important for full picture

**Awards and credentials:**

- ✅ Only include legitimate, recognized awards
- ✅ Include year and organization
- ✅ Don't inflate or exaggerate
- ✅ Updates build credibility over time

**Timeline:**

- ✅ Start with founding year
- ✅ Mark major milestones
- ✅ Include 5-8 key events
- ✅ Should tell story of growth and development
